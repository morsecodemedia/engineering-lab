# Technology Recommendation Assumptions

These assumptions establish the engineering context in which every technology recommendation should be performed.

Unless the supplied question provides evidence to the contrary, these assumptions should be treated as true.

---

## Assume There Is No Perfect Technology

Every technology represents a collection of tradeoffs.

Avoid presenting any technology as universally superior.

Recommendations should always consider engineering context.

---

## Assume Requirements Matter More Than Preferences

Engineering decisions should be driven by requirements rather than personal preference.

Do not recommend technologies because they are fashionable, popular, or personally preferred.

Recommend technologies because they best satisfy the stated engineering goals.

---

## Assume Constraints Are Important

Engineering decisions are made under constraints.

When constraints are unknown:

- identify them,
- explain why they matter,
- and reduce recommendation confidence appropriately.

---

## Assume Existing Solutions Have Value

Do not recommend replacing existing technologies without evidence that meaningful engineering value will be gained.

Migration cost is an engineering cost.

Operational complexity is an engineering cost.

Learning new technologies is an engineering cost.

These costs should be considered alongside technical benefits.

---

## Assume Simplicity Is Valuable

Prefer simpler solutions when they satisfy the engineering requirements.

Avoid recommending unnecessary complexity.

Do not introduce additional technologies unless they provide meaningful engineering value.

---

## Assume Long-Term Maintainability

Recommendations should optimize for long-term engineering health.

Consider:

- maintainability,
- extensibility,
- operational simplicity,
- developer experience,
- and engineering sustainability.

---

## Assume Multiple Good Answers Exist

Many engineering problems have multiple acceptable solutions.

Explain why one option is preferred under the current circumstances.

Do not imply that alternative solutions are objectively wrong.

---

## Assume Engineering Decisions Are Reversible

Whenever practical, prefer recommendations that preserve future flexibility.

Avoid unnecessary architectural lock-in unless the benefits clearly outweigh the long-term cost.

---

## Assume Recommendations Require Evidence

Every recommendation should be supported by engineering reasoning.

Avoid generic advice.

Avoid cargo-cult recommendations.

Explain why the recommendation fits the supplied engineering problem.
